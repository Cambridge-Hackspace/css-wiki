# Wood Dust Safety

## Why Wood Dust is Dangerous

Wood dust is a **serious health hazard** that should not be underestimated:

- **Carcinogen**: Classified by IARC as a Group 1 carcinogen (causes nasal cancer)
- **Lung damage**: Fine particles lodge deep in lungs and cannot be cleared
- **Respiratory issues**: Can cause asthma, chronic bronchitis, and reduced lung function
- **Allergic reactions**: Many wood species cause skin irritation and sensitization
- **Cumulative exposure**: Effects worsen over time with repeated exposure
- **Hardwood vs Softwood**: Hardwoods (oak, walnut, maple) are generally more hazardous

## Protection Strategies

### Hierarchy of Controls
1. **Elimination**: Use pre-surfaced lumber when possible
2. **Engineering controls**: Dust collection and air filtration
3. **Administrative**: Work practices and procedures
4. **PPE**: Respiratory protection (last line of defense)

## Safe Work Practices

### Before You Start
- Plan cuts to minimize dust generation
- Ensure dust collection is working properly
- Put on appropriate respiratory protection
- Turn on air filtration system
- Close doors to minimize dust spread

### During Work
- Keep workpiece and tools at proper height
- Work at steady, controlled pace (slower = less dust)
- Keep collection hose close to dust source
- Don't blow dust with compressed air
- Minimize sanding when possible

### After Work
- Leave air filter running for 30+ minutes
- Vacuum work area (don't sweep - raises dust)
- Use damp cloth/tack cloth for final cleanup
- Empty dust collection bin if needed
- Wash hands and face
- Change clothes if very dusty
