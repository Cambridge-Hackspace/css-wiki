# Wood Plane

## Overview
Hand tool for shaping, smoothing, and flattening wood surfaces by shaving off thin layers.

## Types Available
- **Block Plane**: Small, one-handed, for end grain and detail work
- **Bench Plane**: Larger, two-handed, for flattening and smoothing
- **Smoothing Plane**: Shorter plane for final surface preparation
- [Specific models to be added]

## Parts of a Plane
- **Blade (Iron)**: The cutting edge
- **Chip Breaker**: Prevents tear-out
- **Frog**: Adjusts blade angle
- **Sole**: Bottom flat surface
- **Knob and Tote**: Handles for gripping

## Safety Guidelines
- Always plane **away** from your body
- Keep hands behind the blade
- Secure workpiece firmly
- Check for nails or screws before planing
- Blade is very sharp - handle carefully
- Store with blade retracted

## Using a Hand Plane
1. Ensure blade is sharp and properly set
2. Adjust depth of cut (start light)
3. Secure workpiece in vise or against bench dogs
4. Plane with the grain when possible
5. Apply even pressure
6. Start: pressure on front knob
7. Middle: even pressure
8. End: pressure on rear tote
9. Check surface frequently

## Best Practices
- **Plane with the grain** to avoid tear-out
- Take light passes rather than heavy cuts
- Keep blade sharp
- Keep sole flat and clean
- Use wax on sole for smoother gliding
- Check work frequently
- Listen to the sound - smooth cutting sounds right

## Maintenance
- Keep blade sharp
- Store in dry location
- Lightly oil blade to prevent rust
- Clean sawdust from mechanism
- Check sole flatness periodically
- Report any issues to workshop staff

## Troubleshooting
- **Tear-out**: Plane with grain, reduce cut depth, sharpen blade
- **Chatter**: Reduce depth of cut, secure workpiece better
- **Won't cut**: Increase blade depth, sharpen blade
- **Uneven surface**: Check sole flatness, adjust technique
- **Blade clogs**: Reduce depth, check chip breaker position
