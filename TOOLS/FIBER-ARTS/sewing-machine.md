# Sewing Machine

## Overview
Mechanical or electronic machine for stitching fabric and other materials together with thread. The Hackspace has several sewing machines in the fiber arts area.

Sewing machines have a top thread and a bottom thread that catch each other to make a stitch. The top thread comes from the spool; insert the spool onto the spool pin on the machine.

## Safety
- Keep fingers away from needle area
- Turn off power when threading or changing needles
- Use appropriate needle for fabric type
- Ensure presser foot is down before sewing
- Don't pull fabric; let feed dogs move it
- Unplug when cleaning or making adjustments

## Setting Up the Machine

### Plugging in the machine via the pedal
- Use the pedal that matches the machine.
- Plug the pedal into a wall outlet.
- Plug the pedal's cord into the hole below the knob on the short side of the machine.
- Place the pedal on the floor where you can comfortably step on it.

### The top thread
- The top thread comes from the spool. Insert the spool onto the spool pin on the machine.

### The bobbin (bottom thread)
- Wind thread onto the bobbin.
- Load the bobbin into the machine. Machines are either **top loaders** or **side loaders**, so check which type you are using.
- Thread the bobbin through its case.

### Threading the needle
- Thread the upper thread down through the machine and through the needle. A threading diagram is usually printed on the machine.

## Getting Started
1. Wind bobbin with thread
2. Insert bobbin into bobbin case
3. Thread upper thread through machine
4. Bring bobbin thread up through needle plate
5. Select appropriate stitch
6. Test on scrap fabric
7. Begin sewing

## Basic Stitches
- **Straight stitch** - general seams and construction.
- **Zigzag stitch** - finishing raw edges, stretch seams, and appliqué.
- **Buttonholes** - many machines have a dedicated buttonhole function.
- **Stitch length and width** - adjust to suit the fabric and purpose.
- **Sewing backwards (backstitch)** - stitch in reverse at the start and end of a seam to lock the threads so the seam does not unravel.

## Best Practices
- Iron fabric before and during sewing for clean, accurate results.
- Match your thread and needle to the type of fabric.
- Put pins back in the tomato pincushion when you remove them.
- Use fabric scissors on fabric only - never cut paper with them.
- Use a seam ripper to undo stitches you want to redo.

## Maintenance
- Clean lint from bobbin area after each use
- Oil as needed (per manual)
- Replace needles regularly
- Keep covered when not in use
- Report any issues to workshop staff

## Troubleshooting
- **Thread breaking**: Check threading, use fresh thread, check tension
- **Skipped stitches**: Replace needle, check needle is fully inserted
- **Bent needle**: Replace it; a bent needle causes skipped and broken stitches
- **Puckered fabric**: Adjust tension, use appropriate needle/thread
- **Fabric not feeding**: Check feed dogs, presser foot pressure
- **Bird nesting underneath**: Re-thread upper thread, check bobbin tension
- **Ran out of bobbin thread**: Re-wind and reload the bobbin
