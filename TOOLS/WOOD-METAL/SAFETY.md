# Woodworking Safety Policy

![Cambridge Hackspace](assets/tools/wood/safety/img01.png)

## Overview

This document covers the safety policy put in place for shop tools at the Cambridge Hackspace.

There is a three-step process to be trained on the tools:

1. Read and sign the membership agreement.
2. Read and sign the safety policy (this document).
3. Receive safety training for specific tools.

This training has been prepared to the best of Cambridge Hackspace's ability. You **acknowledge that there may still be gaps in what you have learned and accidents do occur. You will not hold Cambridge Hackspace, the board, or members liable for any damage to personal property or any harm to yourself or others.**

Any member who exhibits improper use of tools may be required to retrain or lose access to tool(s) in the space. **In situations of gross negligence, a member may be banned from Cambridge Hackspace. We take safety very seriously here and we expect the same from you.**

## General Policies & Procedures

This section is a prerequisite for all other sections of this document.

### Member Policies

- Nobody is permitted to use power tools that can harm others (i.e. drill press, belt sander, miter saw, CNC, etc.) on Tuesdays or during any other open house event where guests (not associated with members) will be in the hackspace.
- It is the responsibility of members to ensure that their guests adhere to these guidelines.
- Members are liable for their guests.
- Check the Wiki for non-safety advice.
- Turn off Toolpass for all tools when finished.

### Report All Injuries

- Do not attempt to remove foreign objects from the eye or body. This should be done by a medical professional.
- If chemicals get in the eye(s), wash eye(s) for 15 minutes in an open flow of water before proceeding for medical treatment. We do not have an eye wash station, however bathrooms with running water are outside the door down the hall.

### Use Protective Gear. Dress Right.

- Wear eye protection: safety glasses with side shields, goggles, or face shields at all times, whether working or not when inside the taped off area.
- Do not wear loose-fitting clothing around moving or rotating machinery.
- Remove ties, jewelry, gloves, etc. especially around moving or rotating machinery.
- Tie back or cover long hair to keep it away from moving machinery.
- Wear only shoes that cover the entire foot, no open-toe shoes or sandals.
- Wear suitable gloves when handling hot objects, glass, or sharp-edged items.
- Wear appropriate clothing for the job (i.e., do not wear short sleeve shirts or short pants when welding).
- Consider a mask for dust.
- Consider ear protection for tools that produce a lot of noise.
- Additional foot protection is required when working with heavy parts and pieces.

### Prepare

- Notify others in the room before starting to use tools.
- Safety is your top priority when using the shop. If you are not sure what you are doing, stop and ask.
- Know the locations of all first aid, fire, and safety equipment.
- Never use a tool unless you've been trained to use it safely.
- Do not work in the shop if tired or in a hurry.
- Do not fool around, startle, or distract anyone (not even with a conversation) while either one of you is using a tool.
- Think through the entire job before starting. Prepare prints or drawings with all dimensions and specifications prior to using machines.
- Be aware of hand and body placement when handling material to prevent hand injury and pinch points.
- Use proper lifting techniques when lifting material. Ask for help if material is too heavy or awkward.
- Turn on dust collection systems if you will be making any dust.
- If you do not have time to clean, you do not have time to work. Ours is a shared shop in a small space - put everything away and leave it at least as clean and organized as you found it.

### Use Tools Right

- If you're not getting the desired result, you might be using the tool wrong. Ask for help.
- Use tools only as they were designed to be used. (A wrench is not a hammer.)
- Never use a broken tool.
- Report any broken tools or machines to [training@cambridgehackspace.com](mailto:training@cambridgehackspace.com) immediately and place an "Out of Order" sign on it.
- Never walk away from a tool that is still on.
- Operate machines only with all required guards and shields in place.
- Do not modify tools.
- Do not remove tools from the room. Ours is shared equipment. It must remain available to all members at all times.

### Clean Up

- Wear safety glasses during cleanup.
- Use proper lifting techniques while cleaning up the area.
- Clean up every time whenever you leave an area, including sweeping the floor.
- Clean and return all tools to where you got them.
- Shut off and unplug machines when cleaning, repairing, or oiling.
- Never use a rag near moving machinery.
- Use a brush, hook, or a special tool to remove chips, shavings, etc. from the work area. Never use the hands.
- Keep fingers clear of the point of operation of machines by using special tools or devices, such as push sticks, hooks, pliers, etc.
- Keep the floor around machines clean, dry, and free from trip hazards. Do not allow chips to accumulate.
- If you do not have time to clean, you do not have time to work. Ours is a shared shop in a small space - put everything away and leave it at least as clean and organized as you found it.

## Tool-Specific Training

Each tool below requires in-person safety training in addition to reading this policy. Dedicated pages: [Band Saw](TOOLS/WOOD-METAL/band-saw.md), [Belt Sander](TOOLS/WOOD-METAL/belt-sander.md), [Drill Press](TOOLS/WOOD-METAL/drill-press.md), [Miter Saw](TOOLS/WOOD-METAL/miter-saw.md), [Scroll Saw](TOOLS/WOOD-METAL/scroll-saw.md).

### Belt Sander Training

| Job/Task Step | Hazard | Hazard Control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inspect Equipment/PPE Check/Buddy System/Dust Abatement | N/A | Consider a face mask to reduce inhalation of airborne sawdust.<br>Ensure area is clear in front of the grinder to prevent slip/trip/fall hazards. |
| Start the machine and slowly contact material to the sanding belt | Abrasions/Laceration, Pinch Points, Flying Objects, Eye Injury, Noise Hazard | This tool is only to be used on wood.<br>For orbital sander, material should be flat on plate.<br>Hold tightly onto the material being sanded.<br>Use the base of the machine in front of the belt to support the material being sanded to prevent kick back.<br>Be aware of flying objects caused from sanding activities.<br>Make sure belt is not creeping to one side.<br>Don't press down too hard. |
| Turn off the grinder and clean up the area | Abrasions/Laceration, Pinch Points, Flying Debris, Muscle Strains | Belt will not immediately stop. Do not touch the belt to force it to stop.<br>Wait for the belt to completely stop turning prior to moving on. |

### Scroll Saw Training

| Job/Task Step | Hazard | Hazard Control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inspect Equipment/PPE Check/Buddy System | N/A | Ensure guard is working properly.<br>Wear gloves if the wood has sharp edges or splinters. |
| Prepare wood for cutting | Abrasions/Lacerations, Pinch Points, Muscle Strains, Falling Objects | Lower the guard so it sits snugly on top of the wood.<br>If the material has sharp edges or splinters, wear gloves. |
| Make the cut | Abrasions/Lacerations, Noise Hazards, Flying/Falling Objects, Muscle Strains, Slip/Trip/Fall | Wear a dust mask to prevent breathing in dust and debris.<br>Ensure the area where the cut is being made is clear of obstructions to prevent slip/trip/fall hazards. |
| Turn off the saw | Abrasions/Lacerations | The blade will not stop immediately. |
| Remove cut wood | Abrasions/Lacerations, Pinch Points, Muscle Strains, Falling Objects | Be aware of hand and body placement when handling material to prevent hand injury and pinch points.<br>Use proper lifting techniques when removing material. |

### Drill Press Training

| Job/Task Step | Hazard | Hazard Control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inspect Equipment/PPE Check/Buddy System | N/A | |
| Install the bit | Abrasions/Lacerations, Pinch Points | Be aware of hand placement to avoid abrasions/lacerations and pinch points.<br>Put chuck key back after installing the bit.<br>For larger holes, start with smaller bits and work your way up. |
| Adjust bed height | Pinch Points, Heavy Objects | Adjust slowly.<br>Don't force any movement. If it doesn't move easily, something is still locked. |
| Secure material and sacrificial layer with clamps | Abrasions/Lacerations, Pinch Points | If the material you're drilling has sharp edges, **wear gloves but only while securing material to the drill table.**<br>Clamp material with clamps.<br>Use lubricant if drilling metal. |
| Turn on drill press | Rotating Parts, Pinch Points, High Noise, Flying Debris | Be aware of hand/body placement due to rotating parts/pinch point hazards.<br>Check that the bit doesn't wobble. |
| Rotate handle to advance and retract drill under control | Rotating Parts, Pinch Points, High Noise, Flying Debris, Hot Material, Slipping Hazards | Be aware of hand/body placement due to rotating parts/pinch point hazards.<br>Metal shavings may be hot.<br>If wood is being drilled, be aware of saw dust falling on the floor and into the air.<br>Be aware of foot placement and walking surfaces to prevent slipping. |
| Turn off drill press | Ensure the drill has completely stopped before moving on. | Do not touch the material or the drill bit until the bit stops completely. |
| Clean up work area | Abrasions/Lacerations, Flying Objects, Hot Material, Slipping Hazards | Some metal shaving may be sharp, hot, or wet from lubricant.<br>If metal is being drilled and lubricant is being used, clean any drips on the floor to prevent slipping.<br>Wipe up any wet surface (drill platform/shop floor) during the cleaning process.<br>Gloves must be worn while picking up metal shavings. |
