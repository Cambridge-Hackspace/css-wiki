# Amended By-Laws of Cambridge Hackspace, Inc.

> This page reproduces the by-laws as amended **December 19, 2013**, from the
> member Drive copy. The authoritative version is the original signed document
> kept at the principal office (see Article VII, §8(b) — the Clerk's records).

## I. Name

The name of the corporation, as set out in Article I of the Articles of Organization, is Cambridge Hackspace, Inc. ("Cambridge Hackspace").

## II. Purpose

### 1. Statement of Purpose

Cambridge Hackspace is organized exclusively for charitable, educational, and scientific purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 (or the corresponding section of any future United States Internal Revenue law) and within the scope of Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 180, Section 4 (or any corresponding future section of the Massachusetts General Laws), as set out in Article II of the Articles of Organization.

### 2. Specific Purpose

This section expands upon the specific purpose as set out in Article II of the Articles of Organization.

The mission of Cambridge Hackspace is to foster a collaborative environment in the Greater Boston Area where people can explore and create intersections between technology, science, art, and culture. Additionally, Cambridge Hackspace will provide a workshop and meeting space for members of Cambridge Hackspace and the public at large to learn and apply new skills and techniques from each other, and it will be a forum for the discussion of innovative ideas. Our goal is to share what we learn through discussion and tinkering with the world at large.

## III. Location

Cambridge Hackspace shall continuously maintain its principal office in the Commonwealth of Massachusetts. Its principal office shall be located, insofar as practicable, at the same location as its workshop and meeting space.

## IV. Membership

### 1. Designation of Membership Classes

Cambridge Hackspace has one class of membership: Members.

The Members may elect, by a Vote of the Members under Article VI, Section 1 of these by-laws, to create a class of Trial Members with some or all the rights of membership, except that Trial Members shall have no right to vote on issues submitted to the membership. If such a class is created, all Trial Members shall be required to comply with the requirements established for Members in these by-laws, except the requirement to pay dues. No person shall be a Trial Member for more than three months.

### 2. Eligibility

In order to be a Member, a prospective member must agree to abide by these by-laws, as well as all rules established by the Board of Directors for the use of the workshop. Members are required to provide their name, address, and email address for the corporate records, and they are required to pay the membership dues set by the Board of Directors.

At the time of application for membership, each prospective member shall be provided with a copy of these by-laws. The prospective member shall read these by-laws and agree to abide by them with an acknowledgement of the following form, printed after the by-laws:

> "I [NAME] have read and understood the by-laws of Cambridge Hackspace, Inc. I understand that these by-laws constitute the Membership Agreement between Cambridge Hackspace, Inc. and myself, and I agree to abide by them. [SIGNATURE] [DATE]"

### 3. Rights and Responsibilities

All members are entitled to access to and use of the workshop during the hours set by the Board of Directors. Members shall have a means of physical access during those hours.

Members are entitled to attend any class offered at the workshop at no cost.

Members may schedule and teach free classes at the workshop in accordance with the rules laid down by the Board of Directors.

Members shall have storage space within the workshop.

Members are entitled to participate in member meetings.

Members are eligible to vote on any matter put to a vote before the membership.

Members are eligible to vote for the Board of Directors and Officers, and to serve as Directors and/or Officers.

Members must contribute to the mission of Cambridge Hackspace, and must abide by these by-laws and all rules established by the Board of Directors for the use of the workshop.

Members are required to pay their dues timely.

When a Member's period of membership expires, he/she must surrender his/her means of access to the workshop to the Board of Directors, as well as his/her storage space, unless an exception is made by the Board of Directors.

### 4. Expulsion

Members may be expelled for failure to pay membership dues and for failure to abide by these by-laws and the rules established by the Board of Directors and the Members for the use of the workspace, including any safety rules.

A motion to expel a Member shall be presented by at least two Members, in writing (including email) to the Board of Directors. The motion shall state the reasons why the Member should be expelled. The Board of Directors shall forward the motion by email to all the Members, and the motion shall be presented and voted on at the next regular meeting taking place at least one month after the motion has been forwarded to all the Members. In order to pass, a motion to expel a Member must be approved under the procedure established in Article VI of these by-laws, except that it must be approved by three-fourths of the Members present (including proxy votes). If a Member is expelled, he or she shall forfeit any membership dues for the current period, but if the Member has pre-paid any dues for a future period, the amount of the pre-payment shall be refunded.

## V. Meetings

### 1. Regular Meetings

The regular meetings of Cambridge Hackspace shall take place on the first Tuesday of every month. The meetings shall take place at the principal office or workshop. The Board of Directors may set a different time and place for the regular meeting on a particular month by notifying the members at least one week in advance of the regular meeting time or the proposed different meeting time, whichever is earlier.

### 2. Annual Meeting

The Annual Meeting shall take place during the regular meeting scheduled for the month of December. The date and time of the Annual Meeting may be changed by a Vote of the Membership made according to the procedure in Article VI, Section 1 of these by-laws.

The Board of Directors and the Officers shall be nominated and elected at the Annual Meeting. The Annual Meeting shall also address any additional issues placed before the membership for a Vote of the Membership.

### 3. Special Meeting

Any seven Members may call a Special Meeting to discuss urgent business by giving notice of the proposed date and time of the meeting to the Board of Directors by email at least five days before the proposed meeting. The notice shall describe the issues to be presented at the meeting, and the reason why a Special Meeting is necessary. The Board of Directors shall promptly forward any properly made notice to all the Members.

### 4. Notice of Meetings

The members shall be notified of upcoming meetings, including the Annual Meeting, at least one week in advance by email. Additionally, a paper notice of the upcoming meeting shall be posted in the workshop.

## VI. Voting

### 1. Vote of the Members

**(a) Proper Issues for a Vote of the Members**

Any lawful issue may be brought before the membership for a vote, except issues reserved for the Board of Directors under Section 2(a) of this Article. A Vote of the Membership may only be overridden by a subsequent Vote of the Membership or by a higher legal authority or duty.

**(b) Quorum**

Five of the current Members of Cambridge Hackspace, counting proxy votes, shall constitute a quorum for a Vote of the Members.

**(c) Submission of an Issue for a Vote of the Members**

Any Member may submit an issue for a Vote of the Members, unless the issue is one that these by-laws specify is subject to a Vote of the Board of Directors. To be properly submitted, a short and plain statement of the issue must be emailed to the Board of Directors at least one week before the meeting at which the issue is to be discussed and voted on.

**(d) Notice**

The Board of Directors shall notify all the members by email of any issue submitted for a Vote of the Members at least five days before the meeting at which the issue is to be discussed and voted on.

**(e) Waiver of Notice**

Any issue notice for which has not been timely given (except the expulsion of a Member, the removal of an Officer or Director, the election of an Officer or Director, the amendment of these by-laws, or the dissolution of the corporation) may be brought up for discussion and a vote at any meeting of the Members, if the Members present waive notice by a majority vote. At any meeting, by a majority vote, the floor may be opened for discussion and voting on all new issues on which notice may be waived.

**(f) Proxy Voting**

A member may vote by proxy by emailing the Board of Directors at least 24 hours before the scheduled time for the meeting at which the Member intends to vote by proxy. The email must state the Member's intention to vote by proxy, the issue(s) on which he/she intends to vote by proxy, and the Member's vote on each issue. If the Member submits a proxy vote on some issue(s), but not others, any issue on which the Member does not submit his/her intended vote shall be counted as an abstention. A member who properly submits a proxy vote shall be deemed present at the meeting for the purpose of establishing a quorum, and the Board of Directors shall read and count the Member's vote at the appropriate time in the meeting.

**(g) Voting Procedures**

The procedure for discussing and voting on issues shall be guided, insofar as possible, by the principle of consensus. If consensus is impossible, procedure shall be governed by Robert's Rules of Order, or by any simplified procedure approved by a majority of the Members present at the meeting.

**(h) Majority Voting**

Unless otherwise specified in these by-laws or under applicable law, voting shall be by a majority of the Members present, including properly submitted proxy votes.

### 2. Vote of the Board of Directors

**(a) Proper Issues for a Vote of the Board of Directors**

The Board of Directors may vote on issues regarding:

1. spending money from the general fund necessary to keep the corporation's property in good working order and functioning in fulfillment of the organization's mission, including the payment of rents or leases on the property or properties serving as the workshop and principal office;
2. spending money from the general fund on infrastructure improvements costing less than $500;
3. spending money from the general fund to obtain advice and services for the management of the corporation or the fulfillment of the corporation's mission, or to obtain services to maintain the corporation's property in good order;
4. spending money from any funds donated for a particular purpose;
5. setting membership dues;
6. designating any Officer or Director to sign a contract on behalf of Cambridge Hackspace; and
7. any vote required to fulfill any obligations to ensure the health of the corporation.

**(b) Quorum**

Fifty percent of the current Directors on the day of a meeting of the Board of Directors shall constitute a quorum.

**(c) Submitting an Issue for a Vote**

Any issue proper for decision by a vote of the Board of Directors may be submitted to a vote during a meeting of the Board of Directors.

Any issue submitted that is outside the scope of the powers of the Board of Directors shall be deemed submitted to a Vote of the Members and shall be noticed under Section 1(d) of this Article and voted on at the next meeting of the Members. Alternatively, any such issue may be submitted to the Members by an online poll. The poll shall remain open for at least one week, and the quorum requirement in Section 1(b) of this Article shall apply, as well as the majority requirement of Section 1(h) of this Article. The Board of Directors shall notify the Members, by email, of any poll as soon as it is posted.

**(d) Notice**

Every Director shall be notified of all issues to be voted on at a meeting of the Board of Directors at least one week before the date of the meeting the issue is to be discussed and voted on.

**(e) Waiver of Notice**

By a vote of the majority of all Directors, not just those that may be present at the meeting, the Board of Directors may waive notice on any new issue and allow it to be discussed and voted on.

**(f) Remote Attendance**

Any Director may attend a meeting of the Board of Directors remotely via teleconferencing technologies, which shall include, for example, Skype. Any Director attending remotely shall be deemed present for voting and for the purpose of establishing a quorum.

**(g) Voting Procedures**

The procedure for discussing and voting on issues shall be guided, insofar as possible, by the principle of consensus. If consensus is impossible, procedure shall be governed by Robert's Rules of Order, or by any simplified procedure approved by a majority of the Directors present at the meeting.

**(h) Majority Voting**

Unless otherwise specified in these by-laws or under applicable law, voting shall be by a majority of the Directors present, including Directors attending remotely via teleconferencing technologies.

## VII. Officers

### 1. Responsibilities Common to All Officers

The Officers carry out the day-to-day business of Cambridge Hackspace in accordance with these by-laws. In carrying out their duties, the Officers will act with the best interests of Cambridge Hackspace in mind.

### 2. No Compensation

The Officers of Cambridge Hackspace may not be compensated for their service as Officers. They may, however, be compensated at a reasonable rate for work outside the scope of their duties performed for Cambridge Hackspace, so long as the work and rate of compensation are approved in advance by a Vote of the Members. Additionally, Cambridge Hackspace may provide indemnity and insurance to its Officers for acts carried out within the scope of their duties to the extent permitted by Massachusetts law.

### 3. Eligibility

Only Members may serve as Officers. No Officer shall serve more than four consecutive one-year terms.

### 4. Nomination

Any Member may nominate another Member, or themselves, for office. The Nominee may accept or reject the nomination. Nominations must be made at the Annual Meeting, or at the regular meeting following a resignation. If no one is nominated for a particular office, the current office-holder shall be deemed nominated and may continue to serve in his/her office or may appoint a successor.

### 5. Elections

Elections of Officers shall take place at the Annual Meeting, or at the next regular meeting after the resignation of an officer.

The initial slate of officers designated in Article VII of the Articles of Organization shall serve until the Annual Meeting to be held in December 2013. Their service shall count as one term toward the four-consecutive-term limit set forth in Section 3 of this Article.

### 6. Expiration of Term

An Officer's term shall expire at the Annual Meeting following his or her election.

### 7. Resignation

An Officer may resign by written notice presented to the Board of Directors. The resignation shall be effective upon receipt of the notice, unless the notice specifies a future date.

### 8. Offices

**(a) President**

The President shall preside over all meetings of the membership. He or she also serves as a representative of Cambridge Hackspace to the public, and in all functions where a President may be called for by law or any other outside requirements.

The President is responsible for making sure that the corporation files its annual report and any other papers required to maintain legal nonprofit status by the law of Massachusetts or Federal law.

The President may enter into contracts on behalf of Cambridge Hackspace with the approval of the Board of Directors, for matters that are within the competence of the Board of Directors under Article VI, Section 2(a) of these by-laws, and with the approval of the Members via a Vote of the Members for all other matters.

**(b) Clerk**

The Clerk shall be responsible for recording the minutes of all meetings of the Members and of the Board of Directors, and for keeping the records of Cambridge Hackspace. The Clerk shall make available a copy of all minutes and records to all Members in static form (such as a PDF) on the Cambridge Hackspace website, and shall keep a copy of all original signed documents at the principal office of Cambridge Hackspace.

The Clerk shall also be responsible for giving notice of all meetings of the Members and of the Board of Directors as required by these by-laws.

**(c) Treasurer**

The Treasurer is responsible for the finances of Cambridge Hackspace. The duties of the Treasurer include collecting and recording the payment of membership dues, keeping accurate financial records for Cambridge Hackspace, signing all checks on behalf of Cambridge Hackspace (including all reimbursements authorized by a Vote of the Members), and, together with the President, filing any financial or tax documents required by State or Federal law.

The Board of Directors shall periodically audit the accounts of the Treasurer.

**(d) Other Officers**

The President, Clerk, and Treasurer are the Principal Officers of Cambridge Hackspace. The Members may, however, appoint other Officers to the extent that they deem convenient to carry out specific functions. These additional Officers may include, for example, a Webmaster, a Workspace Manager, a Safety Officer, a Workspace Schedule Master, etc.

In no event shall these other Officers have the power to bind Cambridge Hackspace by contract or to authorize the payment of money by Cambridge Hackspace. Instead, they shall be required to carry out their duties in cooperation with the Principal Officers, the Board of Directors, and the Members.

## VIII. Directors

### 1. Composition

The Board of Directors shall be composed of the President, the Treasurer, the Clerk, and between one and six at-large Directors. The Members shall set the number of at-large Directors for the next year at each Annual Meeting by a majority vote.

The initial Board of Directors shall be composed as stated in Article VII of the Articles of Organization and shall serve until the Annual Meeting to be held in December 2013.

### 2. Responsibilities

The Board of Directors is responsible for the management of Cambridge Hackspace, in the best interest of Cambridge Hackspace and its members, as set out in these by-laws.

The Board of Directors is responsible for the matters listed in Article VI, Section 2(a) of these by-laws. The Board of Directors is also charged with establishing and maintaining rules for the use of the workshop, and for setting membership dues.

### 3. No Compensation

The Directors of Cambridge Hackspace may not be compensated for their service as Directors. They may, however, be compensated at a reasonable rate for work outside the scope of their duties performed for Cambridge Hackspace, so long as the work and rate of compensation are approved in advance by a Vote of the Members. Additionally, Cambridge Hackspace may provide indemnity and insurance to its Directors for acts carried out within the scope of their duties to the extent permitted by Massachusetts law.

### 4. Eligibility

Only Members may serve as Directors. No Director shall serve more than four consecutive one-year terms.

### 5. Nomination

Any Member may nominate another Member, or themselves, as a candidate for the position of at-large Director. The Nominee may accept or reject the nomination. Nominations must be made at the Annual Meeting, or at the regular meeting following a resignation.

### 6. Elections

Elections of at-large Directors shall take place at the Annual Meeting, immediately after the election of the Officers, or at the next regular meeting after the resignation of an at-large Director. At the Annual Meeting, the Members shall first vote to set the number of at-large directors for the upcoming year. Then, Members shall be nominated for the position of at-large Director. If the number of nominees exceeds the number of available positions, the Members shall each cast two votes by secret ballot, and the positions shall be filled in order of votes received. If the number of nominees for at-large Directors is equal to or less than the selected number of at-large Directors, the nominees shall be deemed elected and they shall compose the Board of Directors for the upcoming year together with the President, Treasurer, and Clerk.

The initial slate of Directors designated in Article VII of the Articles of Organization shall serve until the Annual Meeting to be held in December 2013. Their service shall count as one term toward the four-consecutive-term limit set forth in Section 4 of this Article.

### 7. Expiration of Term

An at-large Director's term shall expire at the Annual Meeting following his or her election. The term of an ex officio Director ends when his term as an Officer ends.

### 8. Resignation

A Director may resign by written notice presented to the Board of Directors. The resignation shall be effective upon receipt of the notice, unless the notice specifies a future date.

## IX. Limitation of Liability of Officers, Directors, and Incorporators

Directors, Officers, and Incorporators of Cambridge Hackspace shall be limited in their liability to Cambridge Hackspace or third parties for the performance in good faith of their duties, to the extent permitted by Massachusetts law.

## X. Removal of Officers and Directors

Officers and Directors may be removed from office by a Vote of the Members that follows the procedure established in Article IV, Section 4 of these by-laws for the expulsion of a Member.

## XI. Use of Funds

Cambridge Hackspace may use its funds for any purpose consistent with its Articles of Organization, these by-laws, and its mission.

## XII. Dissolution

In the event that Cambridge Hackspace is forced to dissolve for any reason, the remaining assets and funds of Cambridge Hackspace shall be distributed to another 501(c)(3) organization with similar purpose, as determined by the Board of Directors, in accordance with Article IV of the Articles of Organization.

## XIII. Amendment of By-laws

These by-laws may be amended by a two-thirds Vote of the Members, under the procedure in Article VI of these by-laws, provided that such amendment complies with Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 180, Section 17 (or any corresponding future section of the Massachusetts General Laws).

## XIV. Conflict-of-Interest Policy

### 1. Purpose

The purpose of the conflict-of-interest policy is to protect the interest of Cambridge Hackspace when it is contemplating entering into a transaction or arrangement that might benefit the private interest of an Officer or Director of Cambridge Hackspace or might result in a possible excess-benefit transaction. This policy is intended to supplement but not replace any applicable state and federal laws governing conflict of interest applicable to nonprofit and charitable organizations.

### 2. Definitions

**(a) Interested Person**

"Interested person" means any Director or Principal Officer who has a direct or indirect financial interest, as defined below.

**(b) Financial Interest**

A person has a "financial interest" if the person has, directly or indirectly, through business, investment, or family:

1. An ownership or investment interest in any entity with which Cambridge Hackspace has a transaction or arrangement;
2. A compensation arrangement with Cambridge Hackspace or with any entity or individual with which Cambridge Hackspace has a transaction or arrangement; or
3. A potential ownership or investment interest in, or compensation arrangement with, any entity or individual with which Cambridge Hackspace is negotiating a transaction or arrangement.

Compensation includes direct and indirect remuneration as well as gifts or favors that are not insubstantial.

A financial interest is not necessarily a conflict of interest. Under Section 3(b) of this Article, a person who has a financial interest may have a conflict of interest only if the Board of Directors decides that a conflict of interest exists.

### 3. Procedures

**(a) Duty to Disclose**

In connection with any actual or possible conflict of interest, an interested person must disclose the existence of the financial interest and be given the opportunity to disclose all material facts to the Directors.

**(b) Determining Whether a Conflict of Interest Exists**

After disclosure of the financial interest and all material facts, and after any discussion with the interested person, he/she shall leave the Board of Directors meeting while the determination of a conflict of interest is discussed and voted upon. The remaining Directors shall decide if a conflict of interest exists.

**(c) Procedures for Addressing the Conflict of Interest**

1. An interested person may make a presentation at the Board of Directors meeting, but after the presentation, he/she shall leave the meeting during the discussion of, and the vote on, the transaction or arrangement involving the possible conflict of interest.
2. The Board of Directors shall, if appropriate, appoint a disinterested person or committee to investigate alternatives to the proposed transaction or arrangement.
3. After exercising due diligence, the Board of Directors shall determine whether Cambridge Hackspace can obtain with reasonable efforts a more advantageous transaction or arrangement from a person or entity that would not give rise to a conflict of interest.
4. If a more advantageous transaction or arrangement is not reasonably possible under circumstances not producing a conflict of interest, the Board of Directors shall determine by a majority vote of the disinterested Directors whether the transaction or arrangement is in the best interest of Cambridge Hackspace, for its own benefit, and whether it is fair and reasonable. In conformity with the above determination it shall make its decision as to whether to enter into the transaction or arrangement.

**(d) Violations of the Conflict-of-Interest Policy**

1. If the Board of Directors has reasonable cause to believe a Director has failed to disclose actual or possible conflicts of interest, it shall inform the Director of the basis for such belief and afford the Director an opportunity to explain the alleged failure to disclose.
2. If, after hearing the Director's response and after making further investigation as warranted by the circumstances, the Board of Directors determines the Director has failed to disclose an actual or possible conflict of interest, it shall take appropriate disciplinary and corrective action.

### 4. Records of Proceedings

The minutes of the Board of Directors shall contain:

(a) The names of the persons who disclosed or otherwise were found to have a financial interest in connection with an actual or possible conflict of interest, the nature of the financial interest, any action taken to determine whether a conflict of interest was present, and the Board of Director's decision as to whether a conflict of interest in fact existed.

(b) The names of the persons who were present for discussions and votes relating to the transaction or arrangement, the content of the discussion, including any alternatives to the proposed transaction or arrangement, and a record of any votes taken in connection with the proceedings.

### 5. Votes on Compensation

In keeping with these by-laws, no Officer or Director may be compensated for his or her service as an Officer or Director. If discussing reasonable compensation for services rendered that are outside the Officer's or Director's duties, under Article VII, Section 2 or Article VIII, Section 3 of these by-laws, the interested Officer or Director shall not be allowed to vote on the issue.

### 6. Annual Statements

Each Director and Principal Officer shall annually sign a statement of the following form:

> "I [NAME], with the position of [POSITION] of Cambridge Hackspace, Inc., have received a copy of the conflicts-of-interest policy. I have read and understand the policy, and I agree to comply with it. I also understand that Cambridge Hackspace, Inc. is a charitable organization, and in order to maintain its federal tax exemption it must engage primarily in activities that accomplish one or more of its tax-exempt purposes. [SIGNATURE] [DATE]"

### 7. Periodic Reviews

To ensure that Cambridge Hackspace operates in a manner consistent with charitable purposes and does not engage in activities that could jeopardize its tax-exempt status, periodic reviews shall be conducted. The periodic reviews shall, at a minimum, include the following subjects:

(a) Whether compensation arrangements and benefits, if any, are reasonable, based on competent survey information, and the result of arm's length bargaining.

(b) Whether partnerships, joint ventures, and arrangements with management organizations conform to the written policies of Cambridge Hackspace, are properly recorded, reflect reasonable investment or payments for goods and services, further charitable purposes, and do not result in inurement, impermissible private benefit, or in an excess-benefit transaction.

### 8. Use of Outside Advisors

When conducting the periodic reviews as provided for in Section 7 of this Article, Cambridge Hackspace may, but need not, use outside advisors. If outside advisors are used, their use shall not relieve the Board of Directors of its responsibility for ensuring periodic reviews are conducted.

## XV. Membership Agreement

These by-laws, together with the acknowledgement specified in Article IV, Section 2 of these by-laws, constitute the Membership Agreement between the individual Members and Cambridge Hackspace.
